European freight forwarding still runs on people doing the data work by hand

The category has no product problem. It has a labour problem that nobody has priced.

What the work actually is

01Quoting
Rates rebuilt by hand for every enquiry, across carriers that publish nothing in a common format.
02Track and trace
Status chased by email and phone, then retyped into the TMS so the customer can be told.
03Documents and exceptions
Every mismatch escalates to a person, because the system that spots it cannot resolve it.

Who has it, and why it persists

·Freight forwarders across DACH and Benelux
213 companies in the beachhead. 1,479 in the full ICP.
·The substitute is headcount, not software
They add people when volume grows, because there is nothing on the market that does the work.
·So cost scales with volume
The margin problem gets worse exactly when the business gets better.
€3.4bnPer year

That is the annual labour value of the work being done by hand across the European market.

We are not displacing a competing tool. We are displacing spreadsheets and hours — a harder sale to start, and a far larger one to finish.

The status quo is manual, and it is expensive01 / 08

Agents that do the work, inside the systems they already run

Three design choices, and the third one is the commercial model rather than a feature.

01 Connect, don't migrate
CargoWise and Timocom integrations are already shipped. The forwarder keeps their TMS, their data and their process — nothing to rip out, which removes the objection that kills most deals in this segment.
 Shipped
02 Agents complete the task
Not a dashboard that reports on the work. The agent quotes, tracks, reconciles and escalates — the human handles only the exceptions the agent flags.
 Product
03 Automation rate is the meter
The share of work completed without a human is both the product metric and the pricing metric. Price ramps as automation rises, so what the customer pays tracks what they stop doing.
 Pricing

Why the third one matters most

It aligns our revenue with their saved hours, and it means expansion does not require a new sale. It is also the reason unit economics need instrumenting early — see slide 07.

Why the channel is not optional

Hard capacity guardrail 1 FDE ≈ 10 accounts

Direct growth is capped by how fast we can hire forward-deployed engineers. Reaching 64 accounts on the direct motion alone means carrying six to seven of them — the cost base grows as fast as the revenue does.

Partner-sourced and self-serve are the only two paths that add customers without adding that ratio.

213Named companies

We are not buying lists or guessing at fit. We know the names.

Which makes the go-to-market question narrow: not who to sell to, but how many we can serve per engineer — and what gets us past that ceiling.
